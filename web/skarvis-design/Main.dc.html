SKARVIS · HERMES DETECTION NODE

Know when a drone is over your site.

Hermes listens for the radio signals drones give off: their Remote ID broadcast and their control link. You get an alert, a direction, and an incident record you can file with your protection plan.

Join the pilot list See the build status

Receive-only hardware. No jamming, nothing sent to drones.

Remote ID
Mandatory on class-marked drones in the EU since 1 January 2024. Hermes reads it: serial, position, pilot location.
Direction finding
A five-antenna array listens to 868 MHz and 1.2 GHz links that FPV and long-range drones use, and points to them.
Receive-only
Listening devices need no radio permit in Poland (art. 145 Prawo komunikacji elektronicznej).
Polish incident report
Each alarm becomes a record for your protection plan file.
HOW IT WORKS

Listen. Alert. Prove.

Listen

Hermes sits on a mast or roof. It hears Remote ID on Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, and finds the direction of drone control links. No cameras, no emissions.

Alert

A drone crosses your zone and the guard on duty gets an SMS, Telegram or email, with confidence shown, not hidden.

Prove

Every alarm keeps its raw evidence. One click turns it into a Polish incident report: time, track, serial, direction, pilot position when broadcast.

HARDWARE

The Hermes node

One weatherproof box per mast. The ID version reads Remote ID. The ID + DF version adds a five-antenna direction finder on top. Rotate it, open the cover, switch versions.

Identity receivers

Hears: drones broadcasting Remote ID on 2.4 and 5 GHz Wi-Fi and Bluetooth 5.

Gives: serial number, position, altitude, speed, pilot or takeoff position.

Misses: anything that doesn't broadcast, including most hostile drones.

Direction finder

Hears: control and video links at 433 MHz, 868 MHz and 1.2 GHz, used by FPV and long-range drones.

Gives: a bearing from one node, a position from two.

Misses: 2.4 and 5.8 GHz links (the identity receivers cover those that broadcast), and drones with no radio at all.

What Hermes can't see

Fibre-optic FPV drones and drones flying with no radio at all. Those need radar or cameras. Sites that need them covered can feed a partner's radar into the same map.
